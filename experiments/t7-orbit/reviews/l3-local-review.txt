QUANTIFIERS AND DEPENDENCY MAP

The template Gamma and all exposure weights are fixed; Gamma is a simple
sphere triangulation, n>=5, with a_e>=1/3. Averaged Hamiltonian degree two
gives maximum point degree six. D=2r is fixed before q tends through odd
primes and has D>=12; hence r>=6. The local constants may depend on D, and
joint-sign/product/exposure constants may additionally depend on the fixed
template and weights. Activity sets and sign choices range over a finite
set. No proof here asserts bounds uniform as D or the template grows with q.

Elementary symmetric/symplectic counts
  -> restriction-sign L1 balance, local rank-center counts, joint signs
  -> transverse-locus parity approximation, error O(q^-1/2).
Individual ordered-orbit masses + actual planted density ratio
  -> shared-form image/codimension and finite relaxed gain maximum.
Fresh-vertex exposure + fixed-pair transitivity + event containment
  -> weighted profile cost C, then contribution O(q^(sum g_j-C)).
Integral gains + rational coverage denominator Q
  -> any independently proved positive saving is at least 1/(2Q).
The remaining global proof must establish that positive saving or otherwise
handle its equality profiles; sections 3--6 do not assume it already holds.

REVIEWED CLAIMS

L3-LOCAL-01: Symmetric counts and restriction signs. Supported, with E01.
Manuscript 134--181, equations (3.2)--(3.4).
Comparison: transverse.tex 38--107 and 196--250.
The image parameterization PRP^T is valid even if the image has degenerate
ambient dot product: P is a full-column-rank matrix, and the quotient-form
determinant character is that of R. Counting radicals of dimension u gives
q^[u(N-u)-Nu+u(u-1)/2]=q^-b(u). For two distinct r-spaces, a basis through
their common subspace exposes precisely one shared symmetric block; all
other coefficients needed by their restrictions are independent conditional
on that block. The common block of dimension zero is nonsingular by the
empty convention. Their remaining Schur-complement dimensions are positive,
so the covariance is O(q^-1). A nonempty family then has variance
O(q^-1+1/|family|), including its diagonal terms.
Conditioning on Q0 and Q0-L being nonsingular costs only division of the
nonnegative L1 error by probability 1-O_D(q^-1), uniformly in fixed L.
No determinant-sign match is added to that conditioning.

L3-LOCAL-02: Local transverse factors. Supported.
Manuscript 183--229, equations (3.5)--(3.8).
Comparison: transverse.tex 124--189 and 258--442.
The split maximal-isotropic count includes both families and is
2*q^[r(r-1)/2]*(1+O(q^-1)); its recurrence has N0=1. Complementary rank-r
images prove AB^-1 A=A. Line 201 now states the signs of A and A-B, so the
c0 factor in (3.7) has the correct orientation for X_i-Y.
Writing A=PRP^T gives P^T L P=0 and R=(P^T Q0 P)^-1, a bijection, rather
than only a necessary condition. Pair-sign matches force L split. Given
nonsingular L, (Q0,Q0-L) parameterizes the inverse pair bijectively, so Q0
is uniform subject to those two nonsingularity conditions. Line 220 keeps
the matching indicator inside the error expectation. Thus the normalization
16 times a sign fraction tends to 8 on matches; for two active corners the
corresponding factors are 4 and 2. The exponents cancel exactly, and the
fractions in [0,1] give uniform transverse pointwise bounds at fixed D.
There is no r=1 issue under r>=6. Zero/one-active factors are exactly one.

L3-LOCAL-03: Joint edge signs and transverse assembly. Supported.
Manuscript 231--271. Comparison: transverse.tex 459--641.
For a selected edge subset, one incident vertex has 1<=s<=6 selected
neighbors. Conditional on its lower-right block, the s differences are
independent uniform symmetric matrices. Conditioning them nonsingular and
inverting preserves the product nonsingular law. For N=D-1, the rank union
bound is O(q^[s-b(N-s)]); its worst permitted exponent is 6-b(5)=-9.
The good event depends only on lower-right blocks. Conditioning all other
entries only afterward leaves z_i uniform. Each nonzero Fourier frequency
has quadratic rank at least s+1, so Parseval/Cauchy--Schwarz give the stated
q^-1/2 total-variation bound. Factors on nonincident selected edges are fixed;
edge independence is never assumed. Replacing determinant zeros affects
only O(q^-1) mass, and finitely many sign moments determine the joint law.
Conditionally independent center matrices give a product of local factors;
deterministic telescoping handles overlapping faces. Removing the transverse
indicator only from the bounded sign model is justified. Removing it from
the potentially large normalized original kernel is deliberately not done.

L3-LOCAL-04: Individual ordered-orbit lower bounds. Supported.
Manuscript 275--356, equations (4.1)--(4.3).
Comparison: orbits.tex 91--188 and 264--527.
The common-space quotient and direct opposite-pair sum give the displayed
profile upper exponent. For the lower bound, H_l pairs fully with L_l since
H_l intersect L_l=0. A dual Q_l inside L_l produces a symplectic block. Each
of the three Lagrangians contains a Lagrangian of that block; isotropy forces
its orthogonal projection into that contained subspace. All three therefore
split, and the other pair spaces remain in the orthogonal complement.
Iteration reduces the orbit to a transverse triple, classified by congruence
of a nonsingular symmetric residual form. This rules out hidden extension
invariants rather than merely counting dimensional profiles.
The isometry bound gives every occurring residual orbit a uniformly positive
mass at fixed residual dimension, including dimension zero. Each avoidance
event concerns one independent sample alone. Its failure probability is
at most (q^s-1)/[(q-1)(q^(z+s)+1)]<=1/2 for odd q. Quotient invariance makes
the three residual reductions independent uniform Lagrangians. Residual
transversality forces exact original pair intersections; the successful
common/pair spaces can be uniquely recovered, so summing these choices does
not overcount. Pair transitivity then makes the conditional completion bound
valid for a particular exposed pair, not just for an average pair.

Prospective spans and direct pair spaces give exactly

L3-LOCAL-07: Shared-form image and nullity cost. Supported.

proved. On the common V0, choose F1=0,F2=-D3,F3=D2. Extend compatible values
to each relevant Q_l; the target differences prescribe the other member.
For each F_i there are only two prescribed subspaces, intersecting in V0.

Independent target nullity bounds can be multiplied before conditioning on

declaring the actual three differences independent. Dimension-zero targets
and V0=0 obey the same empty-form conventions.

L3-LOCAL-09: Weighted exposure and exponent lattice. Supported.
Manuscript 502--559, equations (6.1)--(6.5).
Comparison: tail.tex 52--110; orbits.tex 520--526.
A later exposed point is fresh and independent uniform conditional on the
full prior point configuration. LOCAL-04 applies uniformly to its specific
old pair. Iterating gives each side cost. The weak dual of a polygon
triangulation is a tree, so its attaching edges are exactly its internal
edges. The full-profile event is contained in every side event; their
probability upper bounds may be multiplied with weights lambda_t/2 summing
to one. This is event containment, not independence of cuts. Zero-weight
cuts can simply be omitted from the product. Counting faces and internal
edges gives precisely (sum C_j-sum(1-a_e)b(h_e))/2.
Pointwise center gains apply afterward and do not change the earlier
conditional law. All gains are integers, and 2Q*C is integral when Q is
the denominator of the coverage numbers. Therefore a positive saving is
at least 1/(2Q); the number of cuts and the raw weight denominator do not
add an exponent loss. The numerical Q=3, Q_w=11544 example at line 559 is
not freshly recomputed by this review; its finite certificate evidence is
