EXACT DEPENDENCIES OF THE GLOBAL IMPLICATION

The domain is a fixed simple sphere triangulation on n>=5 points with
distinct triangular faces, exactly two faces per point edge, connected
face dual, and discrete incidence color refinement from the two part
colors. A fixed Hamiltonian-cycle distribution has a_e>=1/3. It supplies
f=2n-4, k=3f, positive source degrees and f>n. It also implies point
degrees at most six, used for the displayed pinning-score bound.

For fixed even D satisfying (1.1), the analytic input is (3.1) for every
activity set A and incidence sign assignment, with a q-independent finite
limit and error O(q^(-1/(2Q))). Each signed rank layer has nonzero finite
mass and is normalized to mean one. The coordinate set has fixed size k.
All activation laws, priors, scores and dilution parameters are fixed
before q tends through sufficiently large odd primes. None of sections
9-11 needs enumeration completeness, a classification theorem, a minimum
dimension result, or an explicit finite host.

1. COORDINATE EXPANSION AND REPEATED TYPES: SUPPORTED

Lines 850-865 integrate each independent eta_e. An edge is incident to
exactly two faces, so it has zero, one or two occurrences in the parity
product. One occurrence averages to one; two give the four-corner factor
with c_0^2=1. This also explains why the eventual coefficient is independent
of the prime's congruence class.

Lines 868-899 use product probability spaces. At each source incidence,
the expectation defining that W factor is expanded with a fresh auxiliary
draw, even if another source incidence has the same type pair. Matrix
variables remain indexed by source vertices and coordinates. Conditioning
on repeated types does not identify those variables. The explicit statement
at 917 is therefore correct; it includes all part-preserving maps, with
repetitions and with source edges sent to nonincident type pairs.

At fixed parameters there are finitely many coordinates and activation/
sign configurations. Each coordinate integral tends to a bounded parity
model expectation. Finite products of bounded convergent quantities and
finite averages preserve the error rate. Pointwise bounds on W_q uniform
in q are unnecessary. This justifies the finite interchange at 899.

At 885-914, a disjoint nonempty union of labels has zero moment. Within a
triangle any empty/nonempty pair of edge labels meets at a corner, so a
nonzero face is either all empty or all nonempty. Shared labels and dual
connectivity propagate that choice. Thus only the all-empty contribution
one and the all-nonempty coefficient (9.5) survive. The transpose uses the
same source incidence graph and reversed differences, covered by 840.

2. SUPPORT LAW AND THE NEGATIVE IDENTITY TERM: SUPPORTED

Lines 928-941 use exactly the stated simplicial assumptions. Opposite
faces on two edges through a corner cannot coincide, since distinct
triangular faces cannot share two edges. Therefore the two U-sets meet
only at that corner, giving sizes seven and six.

The proof of (9.7) is general. For gamma=(i,j), availability consists of
the three corners of j and the two endpoint corners in each opposite
face. If U_g uses the two opposite faces, both endpoints of g would have
to be i, impossible for a simple edge. The only alternatives force g to
be one of the two edges through gamma. No numerical fact about 66 or
the original template enters this argument.

The two sign laws at 945-963 are positive rational probabilities. Under
either branch, every nonempty sign monomial except Delta_kappa averages
to zero; the two Delta biases cancel after the equally weighted branch
choice. A label containing the central corner has zero first moment as
well. The overlap moment A(U_e,U_f), however, comes only from the larger
activation branch and is sigma_kappa*theta/2.

For an identity-type nonzero term, (9.7) forces alpha_g subset U_g.
At every corner its two incident nonempty labels must intersect, and
their possible supports meet only at that corner. Each alpha_g therefore
contains all four corners of U_g and equals U_g. This excludes local
diagonal alternatives globally. The one surviving term is exactly
-(theta/2)^k as claimed at 969-979. This is a universal combinatorial
proof; the candidate's finite support checks are additional evidence.

3. ALL-MAP PINNING AND RATIONAL PARAMETERS: SUPPORTED

At 985-998 the score sums are over source vertices and source edges.
Regrouping by endpoints counts source multiplicities, so the square-loss
identity (10.2) needs only preservation of earlier colors. It does not
require injectivity or adjacency preservation.

The explicit bound at 1000 is valid: each histogram has norm squared at
most Delta_H^2, each nonnegative pair score is at most 4 Delta_H, and
there are at most v Delta_H/2 source edges. Hence
  -v Delta_H^2 <= F_t(g) <= 2v Delta_H^2,
while F_t(id)=sum_u ||h_t(u)||^2 lies in [0,v Delta_H^2]. Thus
|F_t(g)-F_t(id)|<=2v Delta_H^2. Integer lexicographic weights L=M+2
make a loss of at least one at the first violated color round dominate
every later score advantage. Discreteness ensures every nonidentity
part-preserving map has such a round.

At 1010-1041 integer powers of integer T give rational priors even if a
vertex score is negative. The thinning exponent is nonpositive, so its
probability lies in (0,1]. Each all-nonempty term receives exactly one
thinning factor per source incidence; it receives no extra factor for
each coordinate in a label. This produces the common normalization in
(10.4) and the score exponent for each of all n^n f^f maps.

The finite competitor bound is sufficient: |C|<=(2^k-1)^m and the
integer gap gives T^(Q(g)-Q(id))<=T^-1 for every competitor. The displayed
strict bound on T makes their absolute sum less than c/2, leaving the
identity coefficient -c. No competitor-sign assumption or omitted
noninjective-map class is needed. This is a useful explicit detail beyond
the earlier source's real-exponential presentation.

4. DILUTION AND ORDER OF LIMITS: SUPPORTED

Lines 1045-1079 correctly leave the opposite-orientation coefficient t_1
unrestricted in sign. In the all-nonempty expansion, an inactive type
has zero moment at every incident source corner. Because every vertex
has positive degree, each source draw from the point-type space must
be active. There are n such draws forward and f after transposition,
regardless of whether their type values repeat. This yields epsilon^n
and epsilon^f, with the empty-label baseline still one.

Since f-n=n-4>0, the fixed negative t_0 dominates. The explicit absolute
bound at 1065-1071 controls both t_1 and t_0*t_1. A small positive rational
epsilon exists, including the separate harmless case t_1=0. With epsilon
fixed first, the strict limiting gap survives for large enough q.

The order D, theta, integer scores/T, epsilon, q is explicitly correct.
Large asymptotic constants are permitted because they are fixed before
q grows. The argument does not allow these parameters to vary with q
without fresh uniform estimates. Every resulting atom mass and kernel
entry is rational, since the signed layer probabilities are ratios of
finite counts. No rational approximation step is hidden here.

5. SYMMETRIZATION AND ALL-HOMOMORPHISM HOST TRANSFER: SUPPORTED

Lines 1083-1101 use independent coordinates in Omega=X times Y. In the
H-product, (x_i for points,y_j for faces) and (y_i for points,x_j for
faces) are disjoint coordinate families. Their integrals therefore
factor as t(W)t(W^T), even when independently sampled labels happen to
coincide. Symmetry, mean one, finite rational maximum L>=1 and scaling
U=V/L give the asserted rational [0,1] kernel with strict deficit.

At 1103-1134, an injective map of the simple source graph uses distinct
unordered host pairs. Conditional Bernoulli independence and independent
vertex labels give homomorphism probability tau. This statement does
not require distinct labels in Omega. The fraction of noninjective maps
is at most binom(v,2)/N. Their true indicators lie in [0,1]; repeated host
edges use their actual single trials and source-edge collapses give zero.
Thus the absolute error bound (11.3) counts all maps without claiming
independence for collision maps.

The normalization p(G)=2|E(G)|/N^2 gives E p=(1-1/N)mu. The displayed N
bound, Bernoulli's inequality (1-1/N)^k>=1-k/N and Jensen's inequality
yield E t(H,G_N)<E p(G_N)^k. Hence one actual simple graph has the strict
deficit. It cannot be edgeless. The strict gap is essential; neither
an explicit prime nor a computable numerical host bound is supplied.
